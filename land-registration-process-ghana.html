Land Registration in Ghana

Step-by-Step Process to Secure Your Land in Ghana: Why Survey Comes First

Many land disputes in Ghana begin long before registration starts. The safest way to protect your investment is to verify boundaries, confirm ownership and properly document the land before making major commitments.

Buying land in Ghana without proper verification can become extremely expensive later. Many people focus only on payment receipts or allocation papers while ignoring the most important question:

“Is this land properly verified and correctly documented?”

A proper land registration process starts with understanding the land physically on the ground, not just relying on documents handed to you.

Step 1 — Physically Inspect the Land

Before making any payment, visit the land with the seller, family representative, chief, or agent involved in the transaction.

Many disputes begin because buyers never properly inspect the parcel themselves. You need to confirm:

  • The exact location of the land
  • Existing boundary marks or pegs
  • Whether the land is already occupied
  • Signs of disputes or encroachment
  • Road access and nearby developments

Step 2 — Verify the Boundaries Through a Professional Survey

This is one of the most important stages in securing land in Ghana. Many buyers skip surveying and move directly to documentation.

Unfortunately, that is how people end up buying overlapping land, incorrect plot sizes, or disputed parcels.

A professional land survey helps:

  • Confirm the true boundaries of the land
  • Check for encroachment issues
  • Verify dimensions and coordinates
  • Prepare an accurate site or cadastral plan
  • Support future registration processes
Ghana’s land administration process depends heavily on proper parcel identification, boundary demarcation, survey plans and accurate documentation.

Step 3 — Conduct an Official Search at the Lands Commission

Once the land has been properly surveyed and identified, the next step is conducting an official search.

This helps confirm:

  • Who owns the land
  • Whether the land has already been registered
  • If there are disputes, mortgages or government acquisitions
  • Whether the land falls within a planning scheme
In many cases, surveying first makes the official search process clearer and more accurate.

Step 4 — Prepare the Proper Land Documents

Depending on the type of land involved, the transaction may require:

  • Lease agreement or indenture
  • Allocation note
  • Site plan or cadastral plan
  • Signatures from family heads, chiefs or legal representatives

According to Ghana’s land registration procedures, preparation of lease documents, examinations, approvals and recording form part of the registration workflow. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

Step 5 — Stamp Duty and Validation

Land documents in Ghana must be stamped before registration.

The amount paid depends on the value of the land transaction. Delays in stamping may attract penalties. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

Step 6 — Register the Land

Ghana currently operates both:

  • Deeds Registration
  • Title Registration

Title registration currently applies to selected areas such as Accra, Tema, Kumasi, Koforidua and Cape Coast. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

Registration processes involve institutions such as:

  • Lands Commission
  • Survey and Mapping Division
  • Land Registration Division
  • Land Valuation Division
  • Customary Land Secretariats

These institutions form part of Ghana’s land administration and registration framework. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

Step 7 — Protect Your Boundaries After Registration

Registration alone does not completely prevent disputes.

Property owners should:

  • Maintain visible boundary marks
  • Keep copies of all land documents safely
  • Avoid informal boundary changes
  • Reconfirm boundaries before construction

Final Thoughts

Proper land registration starts with proper land verification.

Many future disputes can be avoided simply by surveying the land early, conducting official searches and preparing accurate documentation from the beginning.
